Melville Clothing

A 3D interactive clothing store I designed and developed in Roblox Studio, including the environment, displays, fitting rooms, lighting, and shopping features.

Why I made it

Brandy Melville has a cult following, and the way its stores look is a big part of what I wanted to recreate. I wanted someone to walk into this Roblox experience and feel like they were in an actual Brandy Melville.

That meant building the whole space around the experience of browsing. The shelves are filled, clothes are stacked across the tables, and there are fitting rooms tucked away from the main floor. I wanted it to be a place you could spend time exploring.

How I built it

I built the store myself in Roblox Studio. Recreating the items inside it meant working on individual objects as well as the overall layout, so I used Blender to make meshes based on things you would find in the real store.

For some items, I also used AI mesh generation: I supplied a photo of the object and used it to generate a 3D version. The store brings those pieces together with my Blender work and the tools available in Roblox Studio.

The surfaces matter, too

The shape of an object is only part of how it looks. I used normal maps on walls, tables, and other surfaces to give the textures depth as the light falls across them.

Those details sit alongside the clothing displays and warm lighting. Together, they help give the space the immersive feel I was aiming for.

Making it work as a store

I also wanted people to be able to use the space. I coded the mirrors, try-on feature, purchasing, and doors in Luau, giving players things to do as they move through the store.

The fitting rooms and checkout are part of that: you can browse, try something on, and buy clothing within the experience.
